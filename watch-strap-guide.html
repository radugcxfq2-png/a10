GUIDE B / STRAPS

The Strap Guide

A new strap is the simplest way to change how a watch looks and feels. It takes two measurements, one small tool and about five minutes.

Read time7 minutes
ToolsSpring bar tool, soft cloth
ReviewedNovember 2026
01

Take two measurements

Lug width is the gap between the lugs where the strap sits, measured in millimetres. It is almost always an even number: 18, 20 and 22 mm cover most watches. A strap one millimetre too narrow leaves a visible gap; one too wide will not go in.

Wrist circumference decides strap length. Wrap a strip of paper round the wrist just above the bone, mark where it meets and measure it flat. The Strap Size Finder converts that into a length.

02

Compare the materials

MaterialFeelsWaterLasts
LeatherSoft, warms to the wristKeep dry1–3 years of daily wear
Steel braceletCool, weightyFineDecades
RubberFlexible, sportyIdeal3–5 years
Woven fabricLight, casualFine, dries fast2–4 years
MeshLight, breathableFineMany years

Leather is the most comfortable in cool weather and the least happy with sweat. If you wear one watch all summer, a second strap in rubber or fabric will double the life of the leather one.

03

Get the length right

Straps are sold as two pieces with lengths such as 120/75 mm: the long, holed piece and the short, buckle piece. When fastened, the buckle should sit in the middle of the underside of your wrist, and you should be using a hole near the centre of the row. If you are on the last hole either way, you need the next length.

A correctly fitted watch lets one fingertip slide under the strap. Looser than that and the watch rolls; tighter and it leaves a mark by lunchtime.

04

Change a strap safely

  1. Lay the watch face down on a folded cloth.
  2. Put a strip of masking tape over the back of each lug to guard against slips.
  3. Slide the forked end of the spring bar tool between strap and lug, catch the shoulder of the bar and press it inwards.
  4. Ease that end of the strap out, then free the other side.
  5. Fit the spring bar into the new strap, seat one end in its hole, compress the other and guide it home.
  6. Tug the strap firmly. You should hear or feel a small click as the bar locks.

The buckle piece goes at the twelve o’clock side, so that the long tail points away from you when worn on the left wrist.

05

Size a steel bracelet

Bracelets adjust by removing links, held by pins or small screws. Remove equal numbers from each side of the clasp so it stays centred under the wrist. Most clasps also have two or three micro-adjustment holes for fine tuning; use them in warm weather, when wrists swell slightly. If the pins are stubborn, a watchmaker will size a bracelet in minutes.

06

Look after what you fit

Wipe leather with a dry cloth after wearing and let it rest a day between wears if you can. Wash rubber, fabric and steel in lukewarm soapy water with a soft toothbrush, then rinse and dry. Replace spring bars every few years or whenever they look bent; they cost very little and are all that holds the watch to your arm.
