GUIDE A / FIRST WATCH

The First Watch Guide

A first watch involves four decisions. Take them in this order and each one narrows the next, until only a handful of sensible options remain.

Read time8 minutes
LevelBeginner
ReviewedNovember 2026
01

Decide how it will be powered

This is the choice that shapes daily life with the watch, so make it first. A quartz watch is set once and forgotten. It keeps time within a few seconds a month and survives knocks that would upset a mechanical movement. An automatic winds itself from the motion of your arm and has no battery, but it needs wearing most days, drifts by seconds each day and wants a service every several years.

Ask yourself one honest question: will resetting the time after a weekend off the wrist annoy you or please you? If it would annoy you, choose quartz or solar and do not look back.

02

Get the size right

Case diameter is the number everyone quotes, but lug-to-lug length is the one that decides fit. It is the distance from the tip of the top lugs to the tip of the bottom ones. If that measurement is wider than the flat top of your wrist, the watch will overhang and never sit comfortably.

WristCase diameterLug-to-lug, up to
Under 16 cm34–38 mm44 mm
16–18 cm38–40 mm48 mm
Over 18 cm40–44 mm52 mm

Thickness matters too. Anything under 12 mm slides beneath a shirt cuff; over 14 mm and it will catch on doorframes.

03

Choose the crystal

The crystal takes every knock the watch receives. There are three common materials.

  • Sapphire is extremely hard and very difficult to scratch. It is the best all-round choice and now common at modest prices.
  • Mineral glass is tougher against shattering but picks up fine scratches over the years.
  • Acrylic scratches easily but polishes back to clear in minutes with a little paste, which some owners enjoy.

If the description does not say which is fitted, assume mineral glass.

04

Match water resistance to your life

The depth printed on a watch is the result of a still-water pressure test, not an invitation to dive that deep. Read it as a category.

  • 30 m: rain and hand washing.
  • 50 m: the above, plus brief, gentle swimming.
  • 100 m: regular swimming and snorkelling.
  • 200 m and over: suitable for diving, usually with a screw-down crown.

Seals age. A ten-year-old watch rated to 100 m that has never been tested should be treated as splash-proof until a watchmaker has checked it. The Water Resistance Decoder spells out each rating.

05

Pick a dial you can read

Fashion aside, a watch is for telling the time at a glance. Look for hands that contrast with the dial, a minute hand that reaches the minute track, and markers at every hour. A date window is useful; three sub-dials you will never use are clutter. Check the photograph at arm’s length, the way you will see it in life.

06

Try before you commit

Wear the watch for ten minutes if you can. Bend your wrist back: the crown should not dig into your hand. Shake your arm: the watch should not slide. Look at it under a sleeve. The right first watch is the one you stop noticing within an hour and are glad to see when you check the time.
